Built where ideas, agencies, and accredited science meet.

Over years of working across hospitals, academic institutions, CROs, and regulated environments, our founders saw a recurring problem: research ideas stalled because of fragmented execution, unclear ownership, and a lack of integrated scientific oversight.

Academic research was rich in ideas but disconnected from product pathways. Industry programs were pressured by timelines and compliance but lacked early scientific continuity. CRO engagements were often transactional, focused on execution rather than on the scientific intent behind the work.

BioVeritas was established to bridge these gaps. We operate as an independent CRO platform designed to support research from concept and feasibility through preclinical development, clinical research support, and regulatory readiness, under one quality framework, with one scientific voice.

Our approach is built on partnership, not transactions: combining scientific rigor, regulatory discipline, and practical execution so that research programs remain coherent, compliant, and purpose-driven from start to finish.

A study that cannot change a decision should not be run.

Our discipline is subtraction as much as design. Before a protocol is drafted we ask what result would change your next decision, and if no plausible result would, we say so.

That principle shapes four habits: pre-specify what a positive and a negative result look like; include the controls that make the answer interpretable; state assumptions and limitations explicitly in the report; and separate what was measured from what is inferred.

It is not a marketing position. It is the reason our reports survive review with fewer questions.

All servicesScientific approach

Fit-for-purpose beats off-the-shelf.

We do not sell study templates. Every protocol is built around your molecule, your indication, your regulatory target, and the constraint you are actually working against, budget, timeline, material quantity, or all three.

  • Model and species selection justified against target biology rather than convenience or precedent.
  • Endpoints and sampling chosen for interpretability, with statistical input before the design is locked.
  • Tiered designs where material is limited, a small decisive study before a large confirmatory one.
  • Amendments handled properly: documented, version-controlled, and reflected in the final report.
